# Adjacent-combat action probabilities

Frozen 10M seed-0 LOS/rest/sleep model on the same 1,024 validation episodes. Baseline uses sampled actions. No training, hidden enemy information or policy reward changes. All probability summaries below are measured on the original sampled trajectory, not the trajectories generated by the interventions.

## Probability audit

Attack probability is the **sum** of probabilities of all legal directions occupied by visible adjacent enemies. A greedy attack means the highest-probability individual action is one of those directions. These differ when probability is divided across targets. Movement means a directional action not occupied by a visible adjacent enemy. Rest is recorded separately.

Of 70989 sampled non-attacks during adjacency, 9535 (13.4%) occurred when an attack was the model’s highest-probability action. 1705 occurred despite total attack probability being at least 90%.
The model had a non-attack as its highest-probability action with probability >=50% on 61881 decisions, and >=80% on 41431 decisions.

These are action preferences, not proof that every non-attack was wrong. Movement can be a useful retreat and waiting can affect regeneration or enemy timing. The earlier forced-attack experiment demonstrates an aggregate improvement for a particular rule; it does not label each individual decision.

## Target selection

For states with multiple adjacent enemies, all tied minimum-HP targets count as weakest; arbitrary compass tie-break disagreements are not counted as target mistakes.
- Highest-probability attack targets a weakest enemy in 65.72% of multiple-adjacent states.
- Mean probability assigned to weakest targets, conditional on choosing an attack: 64.08%.
- Of actual sampled attacks in multiple-adjacent states, 65.55% hit a weakest target.

## Combat-only randomness interventions

Outside adjacency, the same original sampled policy is used. At adjacency, greedy selects the original distribution’s highest-probability action (not necessarily an attack); half temperature samples from logits divided by 0.5. There is no forced attacking, targeting, waiting or retreat. A full-batch categorical draw is retained each decision, including greedy-overridden decisions, preserving the RNG consumption convention.

## Interpretation

Only 13.4% of sampled non-attacks occur when the highest-probability action is an attack. Most non-attacks therefore reflect a non-attack preference in the distribution, rather than sampling against an attack preference. Almost all probability assigned to non-attacks goes to movement, not waiting. This does not make those movements individually irrational; some can be useful disengagement.

Greedy adjacent combat lowers clears from 867 to 661 and raises deaths from 148 to 315, despite leaving navigation sampled. Halving temperature produces 869 clears, essentially unchanged on this panel. These interventions do not support removing combat randomness as the fix. The previous attack-restricted policy reached 935 clears: explicitly requiring an attack differs critically from taking the model’s highest-probability unrestricted action.

Target preferences are imperfect too: the model’s highest-probability attack selects a weakest target in 65.7% of multiple-adjacent states. About a third of these states disagree even after ignoring equal-HP ties. The earlier weakest-target interventions improved aggregate outcomes, but this audit does not establish that weakest targeting is optimal in every state.

The supported conclusion is that the model’s learned combat preferences, not only sampling noise, differ from the useful hand-coded rules. It remains unresolved whether more training, different experience or a revised objective would correct this. Deterministic repeated behavior may contribute to the greedy failures, but these aggregate diagnostics do not establish that mechanism.

## Validation and limitations

The sampled control exactly reproduces original clear/death/timeout counts, kills and physical turns. Every selected action passes the simulator mask, and every episode terminates. Checkpoint, script and library hashes are recorded. Selected examples include full action probabilities, position, HP, adjacent count, map seed and eventual outcome. The report stores bounded aggregates/examples, not all trajectories.

Summaries are decision-weighted: prolonged fights contribute more states. Outcome-group comparisons are descriptive and do not establish causality. The intervention trials are one model on a reused validation panel; they do not establish general statistical significance or identify an optimal temperature. Action changes alter subsequent simulator RNG consumption.
