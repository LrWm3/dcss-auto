# Sleep-only ablation of the original floor-clear task

This run repeats the original fixed-map experiment with one environment change: monsters begin asleep and wake when the player is within Chebyshev distance six. Once awake, they use the original energy scheduler, shortest-distance pursuit, occupancy repair, and incoming combat. The observation remains the original 206-float observation; sleep state is internal and is not added as a new feature.

Everything else stays fixed: native seed 1057, one map, eight enemies, the original six-species HP cycle, original incoming combat, 18-HP player, 80% player hit chance with 1–4 damage, 256-decision deadline, original reward, PPO architecture, optimizer, batch, rollout length, and three training seeds. The sleep binary is built from `src/learn.cu` with `-DSLEEP_ON`; the normal `learn.so` path remains the original behavior.

## Controls and learning curve

On the separate 2,048-episode suite (root seed 1,200,000), sleep changes the controls sharply:

| Controller | Clear rate | Enemies killed / 8 | Timeout rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Wait | 0.00% | 0.00 | 78.8% |
| Random legal action | 0.00% | 1.35 | 97.9% |
| Shortest-path pursuit | 99.76% | 8.00 | 0.1% |

For comparison, the original awake task gave random legal actions a 45.7% clear rate and pursuit a 91.5% clear rate on the same evaluation protocol. Sleep removes the accidental combat opportunities that made random play surprisingly effective, while leaving a strong route-and-engage controller viable.

Three PPO seeds were trained for 10,027,008 decisions each. The monitoring suite used 1,024 episodes at each checkpoint:

| Decisions | Seed 0 | Seed 1 | Seed 2 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0.00% | 0.00% | 0.00% |
| 1.0M | 0.00% | 0.00% | 0.10% |
| 3.0M | 0.29% | 0.49% | 0.29% |
| 10.0M | 0.98% | 1.46% | 0.39% |

On the final 2,048-episode suite, sampled policies cleared 1.27%, 2.20%, and 0.93% for seeds 0–2. They killed 4.09–4.54 enemies per episode, but timed out in 97.3–98.1% of episodes. Greedy evaluation was lower at 0.20–0.93%. The policies learn to reach and kill some enemies, but rarely complete the full-map sweep within the deadline.

The sleep-only run took 37.5–38.3 seconds of training-loop time per seed at roughly 260–267 thousand decisions per second. This is comparable to the original run's per-seed compute budget.

## 100M extension

Seed 0 was continued from its 10M checkpoint to **100,007,936 cumulative decisions**. Its sampled monitoring clear rate rose from 1.27% at 30M to 3.61% at 100M, with 4.97 enemies killed per episode and a 95.8% timeout rate. On the separate 2,048-episode suite, seed 0 reached 3.81% sampled clear and 3.32% greedy clear, killing 4.93 and 4.81 enemies per episode respectively; timeouts were 95.5% for both modes.

This is slow continued improvement rather than a return to the awake-task regime. Seeds 1 and 2 were not completed to 100M and are not included in this extension result. A partial seed-1 checkpoint was created before the run was stopped, but it is not treated as a completed replicate.

## Longer-horizon evaluation

The trained sleep-only checkpoints were evaluated without retraining at 256, 512, 1,024, and 2,048 decisions per episode. Across the three clean 10M checkpoints, the sampled results were:

| Deadline | Clear rate | Enemies killed / 8 | Timeout rate |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 256 | 1.46% | 4.35 | 97.8% |
| 512 | 1.63% | 4.42 | 97.6% |
| 1,024 | 1.84% | 4.53 | 97.3% |
| 2,048 | 2.52% | 4.67 | 94.9% |

The 100M seed-0 policy improved from 3.81% clear at 256 to 4.25% at 512, with no further increase at 1,024 or 2,048. Extra time therefore rescues some episodes, especially for the less-trained 10M policies, but it does not turn the learned behavior into a reliable floor sweep. The policy generally reaches a similar kill plateau and then spends the added budget without finding the remaining targets.

This probe changes only the terminal deadline during evaluation. The observation still encodes elapsed time as `ticks / 256`, so after step 256 the policy sees values outside its training range. The numbers answer whether the existing policy can continue operating when allowed more decisions; they are not a clean test of a policy trained with a longer horizon.

The full horizon matrix is in `results/sleep-v1-horizon-evaluation.json`, generated by `scripts/evaluate_sleep_horizon.py`.

## What the failed trajectories are doing

To separate combat failure from exploration failure, the completed seed-0 checkpoint was run for 2,048 fresh episodes while recording movement, proximity to live enemies, visited positions, and which enemy slots entered the wake radius. The diagnostic suite uses a different root seed (1,300,000), so its clear rate is not a replacement for the evaluation suite above.

| Outcome | Episodes | Enemies killed | HP at end | Unique positions | Steps near a live enemy | Enemy slots woken |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Clear | 60 (2.93%) | 8.00 | — | 106 | 46.0% | 8.00 |
| Death | 11 (0.54%) | 1.45 | — | 29 | 55.3% | 6.09 |
| Timeout | 1,977 (96.53%) | 4.87 | 17.99 | 123 | 19.7% | 4.90 |

Timeouts move on 82.1% of decisions and visit many distinct coordinates, but usually leave several enemies asleep or undiscovered by the wake rule. Their nearly full health shows that attrition is not the dominant failure. Representative traces show local oscillation after a nearby cluster is cleared while the remaining targets are tens of tiles away. The policy can complete the floor when it reaches all targets; it does not learn a reliable full-map coverage or target-directed sweep from the current observation and reward.

The trajectory instrument is `scripts/analyze_sleep_trajectory.py`; the raw output is `results/sleep-v1-100m-trajectory-sampled.json`.

## Interpretation

The isolated result identifies sleep as a major difficulty increase for this task. The original model learned a mostly successful clear policy; with sleep as the only rule change, it still learns partial engagement but not reliable completion after 10M decisions. Since pursuit clears 99.76%, the simulator and deadline still permit the task. The gap is primarily a learned navigation and target-completion problem, although the aggregate results do not distinguish navigation failures from combat choices within a trajectory.

This is evidence about the fixed-map proxy. Sleeping enemies are globally visible, wakeup uses a simple distance threshold, and there is no wandering, line-of-sight wakeup, hidden map, item use, or resource management. A native DCSS conclusion would require those mechanics and a substantially stronger search policy.

## Reproduction

```sh
make -C gpu-d1 build/learn_sleep.so
gpu-d1/.venv/bin/python gpu-d1/scripts/test_sleep.py

gpu-d1/.venv/bin/python -u gpu-d1/scripts/train_sleep.py \
  --enemies 8 --steps 10000000 --seeds 0,1,2 \
  --output gpu-d1/learning-runs/sleep-v1-10m

gpu-d1/.venv/bin/python gpu-d1/scripts/report_sleep.py \
  gpu-d1/learning-runs/sleep-v1-10m \
  --output gpu-d1/results/sleep-v1-10m-fresh-test.json

# Continue seed 0 alone to the 100M target.
gpu-d1/.venv/bin/python -u gpu-d1/scripts/train_sleep.py \
  --enemies 8 --steps 100000000 --seeds 0 \
  --resume-from gpu-d1/learning-runs/sleep-v1-10m \
  --output gpu-d1/learning-runs/sleep-v1-100m
```

The 10M raw curves are in `learning-runs/sleep-v1-10m/`, the 10M monitoring log is `results/sleep-v1-10m-console.log`, and the 10M separate final suite is `results/sleep-v1-10m-fresh-test.json`. The completed seed-0 extension is in `learning-runs/sleep-v1-100m/seed-0/`, with its final suite in `results/sleep-v1-100m-seed0-fresh-test.json`.
